Project Phoenix — Redesigning Matalan.co.uk from the Ground Up

A 24–30 week solo discovery and design programme to replace Matalan's legacy platform and build a genuinely customer-centred website. The brief looks like a redesign. It's actually a platform exit. Phase one is underway.

The short version

What's the brief?
Produce fully validated, high-fidelity Figma designs and an RFP-ready brief to take to a development partner — enabling Matalan to exit the THG platform and launch a customer-first website. No build work is in scope. The project ends at signed-off designs and documentation.
What's the real problem?
The current site has visible UX failures across search, post-purchase and promotions — but the root cause is the platform itself. THG locks Matalan out of checkout ownership, CMS flexibility, A/B testing, data layer control and deployment speed. Fixing the front end without changing the platform would fix the symptoms and leave the disease.
Who is this for?
A woman aged 35 and over, from all communities and sizes. Value-conscious and style-aspirational, shopping for herself and her family. Primarily mobile. Moves naturally between online and in-store. Post-purchase experience is as important to her loyalty as the path to purchase.
What's the methodology?
8 phases over 24–30 weeks, structured as agile within a stage-gate governance model — sprint-based execution within each phase, waterfall-style sign-off between them. Tools: Figma, FigJam, Userlytics for research, Monday for project management.
What's been done so far?
Five stakeholder sessions completed across Customer Insights, Data, Trading, Customer Service and Product — surfacing eight distinct problem themes with strong consensus across teams. Project brief drafted and in review. Research plan in progress.
What comes next?
Remaining Phase 1 sessions with SEO, CRO, Brand, Loyalty and Dropship teams. Then Phase 2: UX audit of the current platform, competitor benchmarking, analytics review, customer journey mapping, usability sessions, and persona definition.

Background

A redesign with a bigger agenda

Project Phoenix looks like a website redesign. It isn't, quite.

Matalan's current website runs on the THG platform. That platform is the root cause behind a wide range of problems that span customer experience, trading operations, and data quality. THG controls the checkout — Matalan can't own it. The CMS requires developer dependency for basic changes. A/B testing on checkout is impossible. Campaign go-live times regularly exceed 30 minutes. There's no staging environment to preview changes before they hit production.

The business has decided to exit. Phoenix is the programme that enables that exit: a full discovery and design process that produces validated, high-fidelity Figma designs and an RFP-ready brief to take to a development partner. The scope is deliberately unconstrained by the current platform — this is about designing what Matalan's website should be, not what the current system can accommodate.

I'm leading the project solo at two to three days per week, over a 24–30 week programme structured across eight phases from discovery to deliverables. The project ends at signed-off designs and documentation. The build is someone else's brief.

Phase 1 — In Progress

Discovery and research foundation

Phase 1 is the research foundation. Before I can audit the current site or speak to customers, I need a clear picture of what the business knows, what it believes, and where different teams diverge. Stakeholder interviews come first.

The setup work before Phase 1 proper involved building the sprint plan in Monday, mapping a dependency on an existing customer journey map, reaching out to eight internal stakeholder groups, and getting the project plan reviewed and signed off by senior leadership.

Stakeholder sessions

Five sessions completed so far, across Customer Insights, Data, Trading, Customer Service and Product. Three remaining: SEO, CRO, and a combined session covering Brand, Loyalty and Dropship.

The sessions are structured to understand what each team knows about the customer experience, what they believe is broken, what they need the new platform to do, and where the current platform is actively blocking them. The goal is to surface both the agreed problems and the tensions between teams before any design work starts.

The five sessions completed so far show strong consensus across teams. Nobody is defending the current platform. The debate is about what to prioritise — not whether change is needed.

What Phase 1 research has surfaced

Eight distinct problem themes emerged across the sessions, with significant overlap in what different teams reported independently.

Search and findability

  • Poor search technology underpowering the current experience
  • Filters hiding products rather than helping customers find them
  • Navigation structure unclear and inconsistent across divisions

Product information

  • Inadequate photography and model sizing across ranges
  • Missing size and fit information, particularly for value lines
  • No dimensions on homeware products
  • Dropship product content is supplier-managed and not checked before going live

Promotions and pricing

  • Offer codes not working — contributing to 26% of cart abandonments
  • Discount code journey at basket and checkout is unclear
  • Promotions cannot be targeted to specific customer segments
  • No ability to test promotions before pushing live

Post-purchase experience

  • Returns journey fragmented and unclear, especially for dropship versus own-brand
  • No replace or exchange capability
  • Click and collect not tracked after dispatch
  • Delivery tracking unclear; refund timelines not communicated

Platform and operations

  • THG platform identified as the root cause connecting all operational failures
  • No staging environment — changes go straight to production
  • Campaign go-live times exceeding 30 minutes
  • CMS requires developer involvement for basic content changes
  • Checkout is a locked third-party service

Data and measurement

  • Data layer quality in GA4 is poor
  • Third-party tracking inconsistently captured
  • Banner interaction rates unmeasurable
  • No meaningful A/B testing capability on checkout

Omnichannel

  • Site does not support the online-to-in-store handoff
  • Click and collect experience breaks down between online and physical
  • Dropship customers unaware of fulfilment and returns differences before purchase

Mobile and performance

  • Site speed poor on older and lower-spec devices
  • Search bar low-prominence on mobile
  • Live chat obscures products on screen

The platform and operations theme is the thread connecting everything else. Search quality, A/B testing, campaign speed, CMS control, checkout ownership — all of them trace back to the same root cause. Fixing individual features without changing the platform would be treating symptoms.

Project brief and research plan

In parallel with the stakeholder sessions, I'm documenting a project brief to share with stakeholders — a single source of truth covering the goals, scope, constraints, customer definition, and design requirements for the programme. The research plan for Phase 2 is in progress, mapping the methods, tools, and participant criteria for the customer research phase.

Key design requirements confirmed

Based on Phase 1 findings, the following are confirmed requirements for the new experience — non-negotiable inputs to every subsequent phase:

  • Mobile-first at 390px — the primary customer device
  • Owned checkout — no third-party lock-in
  • Self-serve CMS — trading teams should not need a developer to change content
  • A/B testing from day one — not a future phase
  • Dropship transparency on PDP — customers should understand fulfilment and returns before purchasing
  • Rebuilt post-purchase experience — including replace and exchange journeys
  • Personalisation architected in — not delivered at launch, but the system should be built to support it as a phase two programme

Phases 2–8 — Upcoming

The road ahead

This case study will be updated as each phase completes. The plan below reflects what each phase will deliver — findings, decisions, and outcomes will be added here as the work progresses.

Phase 2 — Design Research

UX audit of the current platform, competitor and benchmark review, analytics and metrics review, customer journey mapping, moderated usability sessions, and persona and audience definition. This is where the customer data catches up with the internal stakeholder research done in Phase 1.

Phase 3 — Information Architecture and Content Strategy

Card sorting with customers, navigation design, page taxonomy, and search and filter architecture. The structure of the site is defined here, informed by Phase 2 research.

Phase 4 — Concept and Design Direction

Defining design principles, moodboarding and visual direction, and building out the design system components that will carry through to hi-fi. This is where the aesthetic decisions are made and validated before the detail work begins.

Phase 5 — Wireframing

Mobile-first wireframes for core and transactional pages, key journey prototypes, concept testing, iteration, and sign-off. No hi-fi work begins until wireframes are approved at a stakeholder gate review.

Phase 6 — Hi-Fidelity Design

Full component build and design system documentation, design of all core and transactional pages, interaction design, and an accessibility review against WCAG 2.2 AA before prototype testing begins.

Phase 7 — Prototype Testing

Hi-fi Figma prototypes built for all key journeys, QA of journey paths, moderated usability testing with target customers, iteration based on findings, and final sign-off on designs.

Phase 8 — Deliverables for RFP

Annotated and organised Figma file, journey maps for every flow, a stakeholder presentation, and a compiled RFP pack with written brief. The final deliverable is a document a development partner can build from without requiring significant clarification.
