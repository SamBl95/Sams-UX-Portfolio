About

Product designer based in the North West of England, working across fintech, retail, and property. My work sits between research, interaction design, design systems, and the product decisions that help teams ship clearer experiences.

How I work

Principles I bring into product teams

  • Problem before solution

    I start by clarifying the real user and business problem, then turn loose requirements into options, trade-offs, and a direction teams can act on.

  • Research-led decisions

    I use interviews, surveys, usability testing, analytics, and live product evidence to move decisions away from opinion and closer to what users actually need.

  • Alignment is design work

    I bring stakeholders into the reasoning early, making constraints, evidence, and trade-offs visible before the work becomes expensive to change.

  • Systems over screens

    I look for patterns that can scale beyond one feature, from reusable UI components to clearer rules for content, navigation, and product behaviour.

  • Clarity through ambiguity

    I am comfortable working in messy spaces where the brief is incomplete, the constraints are real, and the team needs a practical path forward.

  • User and business balance

    I try to design decisions that respect the user experience while still acknowledging delivery effort, commercial priorities, compliance, and operational reality.

Background

An unconventional route into product design

My route into product design did not start in a design studio. I studied Biological Sciences at the University of Liverpool, worked around customer-facing and operational problems, and built a property investment business before design became the place where that mix of curiosity, systems thinking, and practical problem solving made sense.

That background still shapes how I work. I am interested in the gap between what organisations think is happening and what people actually experience when they use the tools, services, and systems we design. The best work usually starts by making that gap visible.
